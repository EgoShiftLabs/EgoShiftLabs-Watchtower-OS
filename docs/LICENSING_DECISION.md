# Licensing decision — required before a commercial public launch

The repository is public. Public visibility permits reading the code under GitHub's terms; it DOES NOT by itself mean the entire codebase has an unrestricted open-source license or permits general commercial redistribution. No broad reuse license has been granted in this prototype.

## Options to review with the intended rights holder and counsel
1. **Permissive public license** (MIT, Apache-2.0): encourages adoption and third-party customization, including possible competitor redistribution, subject to terms.
2. **Open-core / dual-license architecture:** a genuinely licensed public community core, with private hosted services, licensed datasets, enterprise modules and support contracts separately controlled.
3. **Source-available custom license:** public source viewing and narrowly authorized customization, with restrictions carefully drafted and enforceable; this is not necessarily open-source.
4. **Proprietary distribution:** restrict redistribution and offer bespoke licenses; may reduce community adoption.

## Release gate
- Confirm who owns authored code, generated assets and documentation.
- Inventory dependencies, third-party data rights and trademark conflicts.
- Decide whether the goal is maximum developer adoption, exclusive licensing or a balance.
- Choose license text explicitly and attach to the exact rights intended. Do not accidentally combine an unrestricted public license with later claims that the same published code is proprietary/trade-secret.
- Keep actual user datasets, personal profiles, commercial secrets and model artifacts out of public source.

Until this decision, potential buyers can inspect the demo and contact the rights holder about a customization or licensing arrangement. They should not assume blanket rights from public visibility alone.
